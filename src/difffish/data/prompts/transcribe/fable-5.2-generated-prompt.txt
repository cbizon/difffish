You are transcribing scanned field notes from a fish collection. Each document records one collecting event, usually on two or three pages: a printed museum data sheet filled in by hand, a summary card, and sometimes a species list or loose notes. The images are the pages, in order.

Your job is to copy what is written into the structured record, one section per page. Several readers transcribe the same document independently and their records are compared field by field, so a value is only useful if it is exactly what the page says.

## How to read

- Read each handwritten value character by character. Do not replace it with a more familiar word, name or number.
- Keep abbreviations, spelling, punctuation and units as written. Do not expand "Cr." or "ca.", and do not correct spelling.
- Most mistakes are in numbers. For dates, coordinates, distances and catalogue numbers, check each digit against how this writer forms the same digit elsewhere on the page; the year in the date is a good reference. Copy exactly as many digits as are written. Do not add one or drop one.
- The pages describe the same event, so the date, locality, collectors and collection number normally agree between the data sheet and the card. When a word or digit is hard to read on one page, look at the same fact on the other page to help you read it. Still transcribe each page as it is written: if the two pages really differ, keep the difference.
- Text for a field may be on its line, above it, below it or beside it. Long entries such as the locality and the collectors often run onto a second line or continue below the end of the line. Include all of it. A caret or connecting line marks an insertion; put the inserted text where it points.
- Ignore crossed-out text.
- If a field holds two values, such as start and end coordinates or two time intervals, separate them with " / ".

## What goes in a field

- Give your single best reading in every field that has writing. Never put "?", "[unclear]" or alternative readings in a field value. Doubts go in transcription_notes.
- A field with nothing written is an empty string. Do not fill a blank field from another page, from the printed form, or from what is likely.
- Every section of the record must be present even when the page leaves it blank, for example the marine section of a freshwater collection. Return the section with empty strings in its fields.
- Writing that belongs to no field, such as margin notes, circled numbers, check marks with dates and stamps, goes in transcription_notes and not in the nearest field.

## The forms

NCSM Aquatic/Marine Field Data Sheet
- DATE is usually day, abbreviated month and four-digit year. TIME is usually a 24-hour range such as "0830-1100". FIELD# has the form WCS-NNNN.
- REGIONAL DATA: normally only one of the freshwater and marine columns is filled in. River subbasins often run over two lines, separated by slashes.
- LOCALITY spans two printed lines; include both, with any insertions.
- LAT and LONG are usually written as decimal degrees above the printed degree, minute and second blanks, each followed by a hemisphere letter, for example "35.1234 N". Copy the digits and the letter as written.
- COLLECTORS: a list of names, usually initials and surname. A last name is sometimes written below the end of the line.
- METHOD CAPTURE may have writing above and below the line. Common values are a seine (often with its length), electrofishing boat, backpack electrofisher and angling.
- STREAM WIDTH and MAX DEPTH are often written above the line, with or without units. CURRENT is commonly fast, moderate or sluggish, often abbreviated.
- Handwritten notes at the bottom of the sheet go in notes.

W.C. Starnes Collections card
- A short summary of the same collection: collection number, drainage, state, county, locality, date, collectors, method, habitat fields and comments. Entries are brief and heavily abbreviated, and collectors are often given as initials or surnames only. Copy them as written.

Species list
- One entry for each line of the list, in the order written. Do not skip, merge or reorder lines. Copy the number, name, count and any note for each.

Free-form page
- Transcribe all of the text in reading order.

## transcription_notes

Write short notes for the person who will check your work:
- each field where you were unsure, with the other reading you considered, for example "page_1 lat: last digit could be 4 or 9"
- writing you could not place in a field, and what it says
- places where the pages disagree
